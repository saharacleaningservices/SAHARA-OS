## D2. Preserving ranking equity — what a 301 does and does not do
A 301 passes most link signals, but it does not carry **content**. Rankings hold only if the new page matches what the old page ranked for. So, before each redirect goes live:
1. **Content parity:** compare the old and new page. The generated pages are intentionally generic. Port any richer content from the old page (local detail, photos, testimonials, FAQs) into the new one; don't drop sections that rank.
2. **Same-day cutover:** publish the new page, add the 301 and update the sitemap in one release. Never redirect to a page that isn't live yet.
3. **No chains:** `/pune/x` → new URL in a single hop. Check `www`, trailing slash and `http` variants also land in one hop.
4. **Update every reference** to the old URLs: sitemap (`/sitemap.xml` lists all 17), internal links and nav/footer, the JSON-LD `url` of `#branch-mohammadwadi` (currently `/pune/mohammadwadi`), Google Business Profile website/booking links and UTM URLs, ad final URLs, WhatsApp templates, Justdial/Sulekha/social links.
5. **Canonicals:** each new page's canonical is itself; no canonical pointing back to `/pune/...`.
6. **Search Console:** submit the updated sitemap; use URL Inspection on the 14 new URLs; watch Coverage and Performance for the top old queries.
7. **Keep redirects at least 12 months**, ideally permanently.

## D3. Monitoring and rollback
- Baseline now: export Search Console clicks/impressions/avg position for each of the 14 old URLs (last 90 days) and current GBP website-click counts.
- Roll out in two batches (7 + 7) a week apart, so a problem is caught early.
- Expect a few weeks of fluctuation. If a page's impressions drop >30% for 3+ weeks, compare its content to the old version and restore the missing sections; the redirect itself is not the usual cause.
- Rollback: remove that row from the redirect list and restore the old page; the new URL then needs a canonical to the old one until resolved.
